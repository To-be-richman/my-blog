title: "Strategy’s Capital Shift: MSTR Sale, STRC Buyback, and Bitcoin Balance"
category: "Crypto"
date: "2026-07-28"
---

Strategy rebuilt its financial structure around Bitcoin once again. The company sold common MSTR shares, repurchased preferred STRC shares, and raised its USD reserves to $3.75 billion.  

*Through the ATM program, Strategy sold 5.43 million MSTR shares, netting $544.5 million; meanwhile, it repurchased 288,930 STRC shares for $25 million.*  

On the Bitcoin side, between July 20–26, no BTC was bought or sold. Holdings remain at 843,775 BTC, with an average purchase price of $75,476, totaling ~$63.69 billion invested. This marks the **fifth consecutive week without BTC purchases**, the longest pause in the current cycle.  

**Key Implications:**  
- USD reserves raised to $3.75B for preferred stock payments and debt obligations.  
- STRC remains central to the new capital structure; the $25M buyback was the **first under its Digital Credit Securities Repurchase Program**, signaling confidence.  
- MSTR and STRC shares rose ~2% and 2.3% in premarket trading.  
- Strategy’s structure is evolving beyond “sell stock, buy BTC” into a complex capital machine.  

**Bottom Line:**  
Strategy is no longer just a BTC buyer—it is integrating Bitcoin into a broader financial mechanism.  
This move also echoes Saylor’s recent stance in the debate over Bitcoin’s future with banks, reflecting Strategy’s shift from “pure holding” toward financial infrastructure.  
Supporters argue integration with banks unlocks Bitcoin’s full potential, while some bitcoiners criticize it as betraying the decentralized ethos.  
The market’s key question remains: will Strategy keep accumulating BTC long-term, or increasingly use it as part of its capital structure?
