South Korea’s crypto regulation debate is heating up again. Regulators are preparing a unified **Digital Asset Basic Act**, while the opposition People Power Party is pushing to abolish the upcoming crypto income tax.

**Translation:**  
Korea wants clear rules for stablecoins and exchanges, but also faces political fights over taxing investors.

**Key Implications:**  
- Financial Services Commission + Democratic Party drafting Digital Asset Basic Act  
- Law aims to unify rules for stablecoins, exchanges, disclosure, and system stability  
- Background: parliament already has 10 separate bills on digital assets, but divisions stalled progress  
- Sensitive points: whether banks should issue KRW stablecoins, and ownership limits for large exchanges — these will decide who controls stablecoin issuance and crypto infrastructure  
- Opposition seeks to cancel crypto tax (20% + 2% local), set to start Jan 2027, with threshold at **2.5M won (~$1,800) annual gains**  
- People Power Party argues stock investors often avoid such taxes, making crypto taxation unfair  
- Petition against tax gained 50,000+ supporters; government still supports implementation after **three postponements**  

**Bottom Line:**  
Korea is moving in two directions: building strict rules for stablecoins and exchanges, while debating investor tax burdens. The key question is whether Korea can balance **regulation clarity** with **market competitiveness**.
