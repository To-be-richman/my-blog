The UK Financial Conduct Authority is laying the regulatory groundwork for tokenized gold, shifting the focus from retail trading to institutional collateral. By evaluating digital bullion for margin use in wholesale markets, regulators are preparing to bring one of London’s oldest asset classes directly on-chain.

---

## 1. What's Changing on the Ground

* The FCA has begun formal roundtables with major banks and market operators to draft concrete rules for tokenized bullion.
* Industry feedback is currently open, specifically targeting how tokenized gold can function as eligible margin for uncleared OTC derivatives and wholesale credit.
* Physical gold settlement remains slow and costly. Digitizing the underlying asset removes these clearing frictions, effectively turning vault gold into high-velocity liquidity.

---

## 2. London’s Strategic Leverage

* As the primary global hub for over-the-counter gold trading, London clears roughly 70% of the world's nominal gold volume, according to World Gold Council data.
* A government-backed task force estimates that tokenized market infrastructure could add up to £33 billion to UK GDP annually by 2035.
* This initiative links directly into a broader push: the UK Treasury is scheduled to launch its first digital government bond—the Digital Gilt Instrument (DIGIT)—by early 2027.

---

## 3. The Real Operational Friction

Extending FCA rules to tokenized gold forces a fundamental jurisdictional challenge. The FCA does not regulate physical spot bullion; its mandate is limited to derivatives and exchange-traded products. 

To make tokenized gold a viable collateral asset, regulators must legally define where physical vault custody ends and regulated financial market infrastructure begins. Bridging this gap without overstepping statutory limits is the immediate hurdle before any institutional rollout.
